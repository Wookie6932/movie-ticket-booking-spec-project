# Participant Interviews

Interview responses supplied by William. Participants are identified as P1 and P2. Responses below retain the supplied wording.

## Participant 1 (P1)

Context: Books weekend outings for her family; primarily uses her phone for calls, text, and basic browsing; easily stressed by rushed online transactions.

1. How do you usually book movie tickets today, and what frustrates you most about the process?
"I usually try to do it on my phone, but half the time I get overwhelmed and make my husband do it, or we just show up at the theater box office. The worst part is getting to the very end of checkout and suddenly seeing $15 in mystery service fees tacked on. Also, picking seats is stressful—I can never tell where the screen actually is compared to the seats, and if I tap the wrong seat by accident, it freezes or loses my spot."
2. The system holds your seats for 5 minutes while you review and confirm. How does that feel to you?
"Five minutes feels like a fire drill! If I’m digging through my purse for my wallet, checking with my family in the other room about who wants which seat, or trying to read the fine print, five minutes goes by in a flash. A giant countdown timer ticking down makes me panic that I'm going to make a mistake."
3. What do you think about being able to book a maximum of 6 seats at once?
"Six is fine for just our family, but if it’s a birthday party or we go with the neighbors, six isn't enough. We’d have to do two separate orders, and there's no guarantee we’d get seats next to each other on the second try."
4. How would you expect cancellation to work if plans change?
"Life happens—kids get sick or dinner runs late. Being able to cancel right from my account before the movie starts is great, but it needs to be completely obvious. Don't make me hunt through nested menus, and please make sure the button clearly says 'Confirm Cancellation' so I don't accidentally wipe out my tickets while just checking the policy."
5. What is the most important thing this website could do to make you feel comfortable using it?
"Make the text large and simple. Tell me the actual total price right up front instead of hiding fees, and if I make an error, tell me in plain English what went wrong instead of giving me a red box with tech jargon."

## Participant 2 (P2)

Context: Uses Discord, gaming platforms, and mobile apps daily; navigates digital interfaces rapidly by relying on layout, icons, and visual hierarchy rather than reading dense text.

1. How do you usually book movie tickets today, and what frustrates you most about the process?
"Me and my friends usually buy tickets through apps on our phones right before we meet at the mall. The biggest pain is walls of text. When an app puts a giant block of paragraph text explaining theater rules or pricing breakdowns, the words just scramble together. I just want visual cues—big buttons, clear movie posters, and high contrast."
2. The system holds your seats for 5 minutes while you review and confirm. How does that feel to you?
"Five minutes is plenty of time for me to click through, but if the hold expires, please don't just dump me back to the homepage. If my hold runs out while I'm trying to read the confirmation page, keep the movie and showtime picked and just tell me visually: 'Seat B3 was lost, tap a new seat.'"
3. What do you think about being able to book a maximum of 6 seats at once?
"Six works for our group most of the time. If someone else comes, they just buy their own on their phone next to us. It's totally fine."
4. How would you expect cancellation to work if plans change?
"I like that you can cancel before showtime, but the screen design has to be foolproof. If the 'Keep Reservation' and 'Cancel Reservation' buttons are identical gray boxes, or if there's no color and icon difference, I might misread them and hit the wrong one. Use distinct shapes, symbols (like a checkmark vs. an X), and strong colors so I don't rely purely on reading the labels."
5. What is the most important thing this website could do to make you feel comfortable using it?
"Keep the seat map clean. Don't use faint pastel colors to show available versus taken seats—use bold shapes, distinct icons (like a filled square vs. an open circle), and clean fonts with good spacing. If I can understand the screen at a glance without having to decode small text, it's 10 times faster."

## Findings and design decisions

The following conclusions are separate from the participant responses above.

- **Pricing:** P1 wants the actual total before checkout. Show ticket cost, fees and taxes before Continue (R6).
- **Time pressure and recovery:** P1 finds the five-minute hold stressful; P2 finds it adequate but wants the selected movie and showtime preserved after expiry. Offer one optional two-minute extension and preserve screening context (R8). The extension is a design decision to evaluate, not a claim of a completed retest.
- **Group size:** P1 identifies a gap for larger outings; P2 generally accepts six seats. Retain the six-seat scope and record larger groups for a future version.
- **Cancellation:** Both want an unmistakable confirmation; P2 also wants stronger visual differentiation. Separate Keep reservation from Confirm cancellation through labels, symbols, placement and contrast (R14).
- **Clarity:** Use short text, understandable errors, prominent screen orientation and recognizable seat states; retain read-aloud compatibility and keyboard access (R5/R19).

## Evaluation scope

These conversations provide qualitative reactions to the shared mockups and proposed booking rules. No task-completion times or error counts were supplied. The revised design should be reviewed again before drawing conclusions about its effectiveness. William's own evaluation and final sign-off remain to be added.
